# FloodCheck user guide

The left panel has four tabs: **Area** (where), **Rain** (how much), **Results** (what happens) and
**Report** (download a PDF to share). A tick on a tab means that part is done. The green
**Check for floods** button at the bottom is always there.

FloodCheck shows where flood water could reach in your area after heavy rain. You do not need any special training to use it.

## Before you start

- Use a phone, tablet or computer with internet.
- Open the FloodCheck link you were given.
- The map is at the top (on a phone) or on the right (on a computer). The steps are below it or on the left.

## Step 1 — Mark your area

**The easy way: search.** Type the name of your village, town, school or market in **Find your place** and press **Search**. If more than one place has that name, tap the right one. FloodCheck puts a pin on it and marks an area around it (about 17 km on each side). You can also type coordinates, for example `-0.45, 39.65`.

If the place is not found, check the spelling or try the nearest town.

**Or draw it yourself:**

1. Press **Draw my area**.
2. Tap one corner of your area on the map.
3. Tap the opposite corner. A pink box shows your area.

Keep each side under about 44 km. To practise first, press **Use an example area**.

Tip: press **Satellite** at the top of the map to see roofs, fields and rivers.

## Step 2 — How much rain?

Choose one way:

- **Real weather** — pick the last day of rain and how many days it rained, then press **Get rainfall**. Past dates use measured rain. Dates in the next 16 days use the forecast. FloodCheck also works out whether the ground was already wet.
- **Choose myself** — tap a quick choice (Light, Heavy, Very heavy, Extreme) or type the amount of rain and how many hours it falls.

Wet ground soaks up less water, so floods are bigger when the ground is already wet.

## Step 3 — Big river coming in (only if needed)

If a large river flows into your area from far away, press **Mark where the river enters** and tap the spot. Then press **Get real flow for the storm dates**, or type in the flow if you know it.

## Check for floods

Press the green **Check for floods** button. The first check in a new area takes a few minutes because FloodCheck downloads maps of the land. Checks after that are quick.

## Reading the results

**Flood risk card** — the coloured card at the top tells you the risk in plain words:

| Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green | Low risk |
| Amber | Some flooding likely |
| Pink | Serious flooding likely |
| Dark red | Severe flooding likely |

Under it is a short list of **what to do**.

**The map** — blue shows flood water. Lighter blue is shallow, darker blue is deep:

| Depth | Roughly |
|---|---|
| 0.1 m | Ankle deep |
| 1 m | Waist deep |
| 3 m or more | Over your head |

- **Tap anywhere** on the map to see how deep the water is there.
- Homes in water, flooded roads and schools or health facilities are marked. Tap them for details. Use **What the map shows** to turn them on or off.
- **Before / after photos** lets you compare satellite images from two dates, for example before and after a past flood, to check the map against what really happened.

## Sharing the results

Open the **Report** tab and press **Download PDF report**. The PDF has the flood map, the risk level,
the key numbers, what is in the water and what to do, ready to print or send. If you search for or
draw a new area, the old results are cleared so you never mix up two places.

## Important

FloodCheck is a quick estimate from satellite data. It can miss walls, culverts and drains. Your community's knowledge of past floods matters: if the map disagrees with what you have seen, tell your local team. **Always follow official weather and flood warnings.**
